A public blockchain is a type of blockchain that is open to anyone to participate in. Anyone can read from or write to a public blockchain i.e. it is open-source. Public blockchains are decentralized, meaning that they are not controlled by any single entity.

Public blockchains are secured by cryptography and consensus mechanisms. Cryptography is used to protect the data on the blockchain from unauthorized access. Consensus mechanisms are used to ensure that all nodes on the blockchain network agree on the state of the ledger at any given point in time

This is in contrast to private systems which are centralized. For instance, an OS like iOS or Android is more vulnerable to censorship and interference. Further, transactions on a private OS are not publicly visible, which makes it easier to commit fraud and manipulate the system. They are also permissioned, meaning that only authorized users can participate in the network with high costs.

Choose the correct answer. Which consensus algorithms are used by L1 blockchains, Bitcoin and Ethereum respectively?

A. Bitcoin - Proof of Stake (PoS), Ethereum - Proof of Work (PoW)
B. Bitcoin - Proof of Work (PoW), Ethereum - Proof of Stake (PoS) 
C. Bitcoin - Proof of Work (PoW), Ethereum - Proof of History (PoH)
D. Bitcoin - Proof of Quorum (PoW), Ethereum - Proof of Stake (PoH)
Bitcoin, introduced in 2008, is a decentralized blockchain platform that operates on a peer-to-peer network. Central to its design is the Proof of Work (PoW) consensus algorithm, which requires participants to solve compute-intensive random puzzles to validate and record transactions. This mechanism not only ensures security and integrity but also incentivizes miners with rewards, fostering a self-sustaining system enabling peer-to-peer payments without middlemen.

Initiated in 2013, Ethereum emerged as a pioneering platform, elevating the potential of blockchain beyond Bitcoin's primary focus on peer-to-peer transactions and value storage. In 2022, in a pivotal industry event dubbed "The Merge," Ethereum transitioned from the energy-intensive Proof of Work (PoW) to the more sustainable Proof of Stake (PoS) consensus model. This shift not only showcased technological evolution but also addressed the broader perception of blockchain's environmental impact. Instead of miners in PoW,  validators are chosen to validate and propose blocks in PoS,  based on the amount of native coins they've staked or offered as collateral.

Which statement accurately describes the relationship between ERC standards (Ethereum request for Comments) and EIPs in the Ethereum ecosystem?

A. ERC standards are developed independently and primarily deal with smart contract optimization
B. ERC standards, like ERC-20 and ERC-721, are types of EIPs that specifically define token standards on Ethereum 
C. EIPs are exclusively for updating Ethereum's core protocol, whereas ERC is a standard for Ethereum's governance model 
D. ERC is Ethereum's consensus algorithm, while EIPs are a subset of ERC
EIP stands for Ethereum Improvement Proposal, and it's a design document that provides a concise technical specification of a feature or change and the rationale behind it for the Ethereum platform. ERC, which means Ethereum Request for Comments, is a subset of EIPs. ERCs specifically propose standards related to tokens. Notable examples include the ERC-20 standard, which has become the blueprint for creating fungible tokens (like many of the cryptocurrencies), and the ERC-721 standard, which is used for non-fungible tokens (NFTs). Thus, while all ERCs are EIPs, not all EIPs are ERCs.

Which of the following reasons explains why specialized sandbox environments called testnets or test networks are crucial for blockchain platforms?

A. They help in developing and deploying smart contracts
B. They help make blockchain platforms energy efficient
C. They provide environments to simulate the blockchain network for testing purposes before deploying on the mainnet, which involves real world value 
D. They are one of the protocols to be followed in the blockchain industry
Testnets are crucial for blockchain development, providing a safe, cost-effective sandbox for developers. Deploying untested code on the mainnet, given blockchain's immutable nature, can lead to irreversible bugs or vulnerabilities, risking fund loss. On platforms like Ethereum, interactions, even during testing, entail real gas fees; these costs can accumulate. Testnets alleviate this by offering simulated test tokens for experimentation, ensuring that applications are both economically tested and primed for mainnet deployment without real-world consequences.
