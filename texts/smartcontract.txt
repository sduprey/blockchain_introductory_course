What is the term used to refer to the fee paid by end users to execute a function in a smart contract such as transferring tokens, voting in a DAO or minting an NFT?

A. Maker Fee
B. Taker Fee
C. Gas Fee 
D. MEV (maximal extractable value)
On smart contract networks that double up as L1 blockchains like Ethereum and Shardeum, the term "gas" is used to describe the unit of computational effort required to execute operations, like making a transfer or executing a smart contract. The "gas fee" is the cost of this effort and is determined by multiplying the gas used by the gas price set by the sender. This gas fee is essentially the transaction fee on the network. Gas fee, additionally, can serve as a measure to prevent spammy or malicious behavior on the network by making it costly to conduct such activities.

Note, transaction fees are a broader term applicable to blockchain networks. They represent the cost associated with making a transaction on the network. This fee compensates the network validators or miners for their work in processing and confirming transactions. “Gas fees" are a type of transaction fee,  while "transaction fee" is more general and can be applied to any blockchain network transaction.

B. Database management system

Solidity is a high-level, statically-typed programming language specifically designed for writing smart contracts on Ethereum and other blockchain platforms that support the Ethereum Virtual Machine (EVM). Unlike front-end tools or databases, Solidity provides the means to encode the logic, rules, and data structures of decentralized applications directly on the blockchain. Its syntax is similar to that of JavaScript, but it includes types, interfaces, and complex data structures, making it powerful and optimized for blockchain development. Furthermore, Solidity's functionality is pivotal in ensuring trustworthiness, transparency, and immutability in dapps.

Which of the following is the MOST accurate definition of gas estimate and gas limit?

A. Gas estimate is the estimated amount of gas that a transaction will require, while gas limit is the maximum amount of gas that a user is willing to spend on a transaction 
B. Gas estimate is the amount of gas that a transaction has already used, while gas limit is the maximum amount of gas that a transaction can use
C. Gas estimate is the maximum amount of gas that a user is willing to spend on a transaction, while gas limit is the estimated amount of gas that a transaction will require
D. Gas estimate is the amount of gas that a transaction has used, while gas limit is the estimated amount of gas that a transaction will require
The gas estimate is the amount of gas that a wallet or other tool estimates that a transaction will require. The gas limit is the maximum amount of gas that a user is willing to spend on a transaction. If a transaction requires more gas than the gas limit, the transaction will fail. The gas estimate is not always accurate, and it can be affected by a number of factors, such as the complexity of the transaction and the current state of the network. However, tools like Etherscan, MetaMask, Alchemy are useful tools for estimating the cost of a transaction.

The gas limit is important because it prevents users from spending more than they are willing on a transaction. It also helps to protect the network from malicious transactions that are designed to consume a lot of gas.

Example: A user is sending a simple ETH transfer. The wallet estimates that the transaction will require 21,000 gas. The user sets the gas limit to 21,000 gas. The transaction is successful and the user pays 21,000 gas in fees. However, if the user had set the gas limit to 10,000 gas, the transaction would have failed and the user would have lost the gas that they had already paid.

What is the significant advantage of using oracles in smart contracts?

A. Allow contracts to retrieve external world data 
B. Increase the speed of transactions
C. Reduce gas fees
D. Encrypt and decrypt contract data
Let’s take the weather example again. Imagine a decentralized insurance platform that compensates users if their flight is canceled due to bad weather. The smart contract governing this insurance would need real-time weather data to determine whether a claim is valid or not. However, the smart contract doesn't inherently know the current weather conditions. This is where the oracle comes in. The oracle fetches the weather data from a trusted weather reporting website or service and inputs it into the smart contract. With the data provided by the oracle, the smart contract can verify if conditions warrant compensation for a weather-caused cancellation and then decide to release the funds if necessary.
